# 💡 LEARNING TIP

## One Search, Two Benefits

When you research a topic for **IELTS Writing Task 2**, don't begin with random searches.

### 🎯 Start with Cambridge Reading Sources

Whenever possible, first look at the **authentic sources used by Cambridge IELTS for its Reading texts**.

Why?

Because this simple strategy gives you **two learning benefits at the same time**.

### ✦ Benefit 1 · Prepare for Writing

By reading an authentic article related to your Writing topic, you can:

* build background knowledge
* discover new ideas and perspectives
* understand causes, effects, problems and solutions
* find relevant examples
* develop your own position
* learn topic-specific vocabulary and useful language

### ✦ Benefit 2 · Improve Your Reading

At the same time, you are becoming more familiar with the kinds of authentic sources Cambridge uses for IELTS Reading.

You may encounter sources such as:

**New Scientist · The Guardian · The Conversation · University of Cambridge · World History Encyclopedia · Literary Review**

So your research is not only helping you **write about the topic**.

It is also giving you more exposure to **authentic English reading**.

---

## 🧠 THE KEY IDEA

> **Don't search for an IELTS answer. Search for the real-world knowledge behind the topic.**

And whenever possible:

> **Start with sources Cambridge uses for Reading.**

# 💡 LEARNING TIP

## How to Extract Writing Ideas from an Authentic Article

Finding a good article is only the beginning.

The goal is **not to copy ideas from the article and put them into your IELTS essay.**

The goal is to use the article to **build your own knowledge, generate ideas, and develop your own arguments.**

---

# 🗺️ IDEA EXTRACTION ROAD MAP

### ARTICLE

↓

### 1 · UNDERSTAND

**What is the article mainly about?**

Identify:

* the main topic
* the central issue
* the key questions
* the main problem or development

↓

### 2 · NOTICE THE ISSUES

**What important issues does the article raise?**

Look for:

**Causes · Effects · Problems · Benefits · Risks · Solutions · Changes · Different perspectives**

Don't try to collect everything.

**Select what could be useful for your Writing topic.**

↓

### 3 · COLLECT KNOWLEDGE

**What does the article teach you?**

Extract:

* important facts
* explanations
* processes
* examples
* real-world situations
* relationships between causes and effects

At this stage, you are **building background knowledge**, not writing your essay.

↓

### 4 · TURN KNOWLEDGE INTO QUESTIONS

Ask:

* Why does this happen?
* What causes it?
* Who is affected?
* What are the consequences?
* What are the benefits?
* What are the disadvantages?
* What could be done?
* Who should be responsible?
* Could there be another perspective?

This is where **information starts becoming ideas.**

↓

### 5 · GENERATE POSSIBLE WRITING IDEAS

Turn what you learned into possible arguments.

For example:

**ARTICLE INFORMATION**
→ The article explains that X is increasing because of Y.

**POSSIBLE WRITING IDEA**
→ One reason for X is Y.

Or:

**ARTICLE INFORMATION**
→ The article describes a negative consequence of X.

**POSSIBLE WRITING IDEA**
→ X can have a negative impact on...

The article gives you the **knowledge**.

You develop the **argument**.

↓

### 6 · DEVELOP THE IDEA

For each useful idea, ask:

**WHAT?**
What is the argument?

**WHY?**
Why is it true or important?

**HOW?**
How does this happen?

**RESULT?**
What does it lead to?

**EXAMPLE?**
Can I illustrate it with a real-world example?

This transforms a simple idea into a **developed IELTS argument**.

↓

### 7 · ADD YOUR OWN THINKING

Now step away from the article.

Ask:

> **Do I agree with this? Why?**

> **Is there another side?**

> **Would this apply everywhere?**

> **What might be a limitation or exception?**

> **What is my own position?**

This is essential.

**The article provides input.
You provide the thinking.**

↓

### 8 · BUILD YOUR WRITING PLAN

Now select the strongest ideas.

**TOPIC**
↓
**POSITION / THESIS**
↓
**MAIN IDEA 1**
→ Why?
→ How?
→ Example?
↓
**MAIN IDEA 2**
→ Why?
→ How?
→ Example?
↓
**COUNTERPOINT / QUALIFICATION** *(when relevant)*
↓
**CONCLUSION**

## 🧩 Worked Example: From Article → Writing Idea → Argument

Writing Topic:
-
> **Some people believe that the increasing use of AI will have a negative impact on employment. To what extent do you agree or disagree?**

فرض کنیم برای تحقیق به مقاله‌ای مثل “Why AI Can't Solve Everything” از The Conversation رسیدیم .

## 🗺️ STEP 1 — Understand the Article

#### Article topic:
AI and its limitations

#### Central issue:
Can AI replace human capabilities completely?

#### Key question:
Are there things humans can do that AI cannot easily replicate?
## 🔎 STEP 2 — Notice the Issues

از متن، چند issue پیدا میکنیم:
```
AI can perform certain tasks efficiently.
AI has limitations.
Some human abilities are difficult to replicate.
Human judgement and understanding remain important.
Technology does not necessarily eliminate the need for humans.
```
فعلاً هیچ essay نمی‌نویسیم.

فقط knowledge جمع می‌کنیم.

## 🧠 STEP 3 — Extract Useful Knowledge

حالا یک observation از مقاله را انتخاب می‌کنیم:

> **AI is powerful at performing certain tasks, but it has limitations when tasks require human judgement and understanding.**

این هنوز Writing argument نیست.

این فقط knowledge است.
## ❓ STEP 4 — Turn Knowledge into Questions

حالا دانشجو شروع می‌کند به سؤال پرسیدن:

**Why does this matter for employment?**

→ Because not all jobs consist of repetitive tasks.

**What kinds of jobs may be less easily replaced?**

→ Jobs requiring judgement, interpersonal interaction and complex decision-making.

**What could happen to these jobs?**

→ Humans may continue to play an important role even when AI is widely used.

حالا داریم به idea نزدیک می‌شویم.
## 💡 STEP 5 — Generate a Writing Idea

از knowledge قبلی:

##### ARTICLE KNOWLEDGE

AI has limitations in tasks requiring human judgement.

↓

##### WRITING IDEA

**AI is unlikely to completely replace workers in occupations that depend heavily on human judgement and interpersonal skills.**

این دیگر یک IELTS Writing idea است.

اما هنوز argument کامل نیست.

## 🧱 STEP 6 — Develop the Argument

حالا همان WHAT → WHY → HOW → RESULT → EXAMPLE را اجرا می‌کنیم.

| Layer        | Development                                                                                                 |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **WHAT?**    | AI may not completely replace workers in jobs requiring human judgement.                                    |
| **WHY?**     | Such jobs involve interpreting complex situations and responding to human needs.                            |
| **HOW?**     | AI can process information, but human workers can adapt their decisions to individual circumstances.        |
| **RESULT?**  | Therefore, AI may change these jobs rather than eliminate them entirely.                                    |
| **EXAMPLE?** | Teachers, doctors and social workers often need to respond to individual emotional or social circumstances. |


حالا یک developed argument داریم.

## 🧠 STEP 7 — Add YOUR Thinking

اینجا باید از مقاله فاصله بگیریم.

سؤال:

**Does this mean AI will have no negative effect on employment?**

نه.

ممکن است AI بعضی وظایف را حذف کند، حتی اگر کل شغل را حذف نکند.

پس دانشجو می‌تواند argument را qualify کند:

**AI may not completely eliminate many professions, but it could still reduce the number of workers required by automating certain tasks.**

این قسمت دیگر صرفاً article summary نیست.

این critical thinking خود دانشجو است.

## ✍️ STEP 8 — Turn It into an IELTS Paragraph

حالا تمام مراحل قبلی تبدیل می‌شود به یک paragraph:

>One reason why AI may not completely eliminate employment is that many occupations require human judgement and interpersonal skills. Although artificial intelligence can process large amounts of information and perform specific tasks efficiently, it may struggle to respond appropriately to complex individual circumstances. As a result, AI is more likely to transform certain professions than replace the people working in them entirely. For example, teachers, doctors and social workers often need to make decisions based not only on available information but also on individual emotional and social needs. Nevertheless, AI could still reduce the demand for labour by automating some of the routine tasks within these professions.
